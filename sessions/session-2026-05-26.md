# Session – 2026-05-26

## Topics covered
- OLTP schema design with normalized tables, foreign keys, indexes, and triggers for maintaining assignment history and handling changing operational data.
- Historical tracking concepts using validity periods and automatic logging, followed by dimensional modeling with star schemas, dimension tables, fact tables, surrogate keys, grain definition, and ETL pipelines for analytical workloads.
- Building data warehouse structures, separating transactional systems from analytical systems, and designing aggregation processes that preserve historical ownership information even after reassignment events.
- Applying ETL concepts with extraction, transformation, grouping, and loading processes, together with verification queries that connect dimensions and facts to validate analytical results.

## What I understood
- The separation between OLTP and OLAP systems and why transactional schemas are not ideal for analytical queries.
- How triggers and history tables allow tracking temporal changes and why validity ranges are necessary when assignments change over time.
- The purpose of dimensions, facts, surrogate keys, and grain definitions in a star schema, and how these structures simplify reporting and KPI calculations.
- How ETL processes reconstruct historical events by determining which entity was responsible at a specific point in time rather than relying on the current state of the source tables.

## What is still confusing
- Choosing the correct grain for fact tables and understanding how different grain definitions affect future reporting capabilities.
- Handling more complex historical scenarios where multiple reassignments occur between creation and completion events.
- Deciding when a metric belongs inside the fact table versus being calculated dynamically during reporting.

## Questions
- How would slowly changing dimensions be implemented when user attributes such as teams or roles change over time?
- What strategies are commonly used when fact tables become very large and require incremental ETL instead of complete reloads?
- How do production ETL tools handle failures, retries, and data consistency when loading warehouse tables?
